# Competitor Research — Erasmus Onboarding Website (unibz)

This document maps existing solutions ("competitors") at three levels:

1. Europe-wide Erasmus platforms
2. Onboarding apps used by other universities
3. What already exists locally at unibz / Bolzano

---

## 1. Europe-wide Erasmus platforms

### Erasmus+ App (official, EU)
- **Who:** Developed by a consortium of Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), the European University Foundation (EUF) and the Erasmus Student Network (ESN). First launched in 2017, relaunched as the "single point of entry" to Erasmus+ from 2020–2021.
- **Platforms:** iOS, Android, web ([erasmusapp.eu](https://erasmusapp.eu/)).
- **Features:**
  - Structured information on Erasmus+ programmes, in many EU languages
  - "Mobility Journey" with interactive checklists for before / during / after the stay
  - Integration with the Online Learning Agreement and OLS language support
  - Tips and stories from other students (Tip Collector)
  - Notifications from student services
  - Accommodation module with curated housing offers
  - ESN services: ESNcard discounts and local ESN events
- **Strengths:** Official, free, backed by the European Commission, covers the administrative side.
- **Weaknesses / gap for us:** Generic across all of Europe. No Bolzano- or unibz-specific practical knowledge (trilingual context, local bus pass, local paperwork, campus specifics).

### Erasmus Play
- **What:** App and website positioned as a reference app for Erasmus students; covers 500+ destinations and partners with 180+ European universities.
- **Features:**
  - Verified accommodation search engine (compare, filter, alerts, booking requests)
  - Meet other Erasmus students going to the same destination before arrival
  - Automatic access to destination groups and chats
  - City guides
- **Strengths:** Strong on housing and pre-arrival social connection.
- **Weaknesses / gap for us:** Business model centred on accommodation; not a guide to university procedures or first-week tasks.

### Erasmusu
- **What:** Housing marketplace plus community (forums, blogs, student experiences, city pages). Founded 2009 in Murcia, Spain; revenue from commission on bookings.
- **Bolzano presence:** Has a Bolzano section — forum, accommodation listings, experiences, "what to see", where to eat.
- **Weaknesses / gap for us:** Bolzano content is old and thin (the English forum thread is about 5 years old; many experience reports are 8–11 years old). Not maintained by anyone local.

### Eurasmus (defunct)
- **What:** Website founded in 2014 offering accommodation, internships and city guides with secure online payment.
- **Status:** No longer operating.
- **Lesson for us:** Useful cautionary case — platforms that depend on a broad marketplace without a sustained community or institutional backing can disappear. Think about who maintains our content after the course ends.

## 2. Onboarding apps used by other universities

### Goin' (commercial, used by many universities)
- **Who uses it:** University of Oslo, University of Antwerp, Tampere University / TAMK, SLU (Sweden), Lund, NMBU, DCU, Maynooth, Erasmus University Rotterdam, UFV (Canada), and others.
- **Origin:** Started as a classroom idea and became a startup.
- **Features:**
  - Social network for admitted international students before arrival
  - 1-to-1 connections with new and current students
  - Interest groups, home-country groups, study-programme groups (e.g. housing groups)
  - Arrival guide
  - World map showing where fellow students come from
  - Campus/city map with points of interest (e.g. Lund: student nations, bars, study spots)
- **Strengths:** Very strong on the social side and on the pre-arrival period.
- **Weaknesses / gap for us:** Mainly social; universities often say staff are not active in it (SLU) and that they take no responsibility for information shared there (Tampere). Information quality depends on students. Paid B2B product — unibz does not appear to use it.

### NCU OnBoard — Nicolaus Copernicus University (Toruń, Poland)
- Digital guide for international students and staff, available as a website and app.
- Covers administrative procedures, living in Poland and how the university works.
- Organised in modules (before arrival, during the stay).
- **Relevance:** Closest example of a university-specific, practical guide app — similar to what we want, but built by the university itself.

### EHUwelcome — University of the Basque Country (Spain)
- App covering university services, accommodation on the campuses, transport, Spanish and Basque language courses, and basic information about the country.
- **Relevance:** Good model for a region with its own language situation (comparable to South Tyrol's Italian / German / Ladin context).

### iCent app (several Canadian colleges, e.g. University of Lethbridge, Fleming College)
- Pre-arrival and post-arrival checklists and need-to-know information for international students.

### Other university apps
- **Unifr app (University of Fribourg, Switzerland):** official university app that guides incoming exchange students.
- **Welcome to OVGU (Magdeburg, Germany):** companion app for new and international students, with event registration through the International Office.
- **Welcome to AGH UST (Kraków, Poland):** app for international students arriving on campus for the first time.
- **Sheridan College (Canada):** pre-departure checklists, airport arrival help, campus maps.
- **Université Paris-Saclay:** app creating a personalised plan to navigate French administrative procedures.

### Student projects (very close to ours)
- **"Global Tigers" — University of Memphis CS capstone (Spring 2024):** onboarding app for incoming international students with a checklist of onboarding tasks and an interactive campus map. Almost the same scope as our project — useful comparison point for the report.

### Buddy programmes (non-digital competitor)
- Run by ESN sections and universities across Europe (e.g. USI Lugano with ESN Lugano, Graz, Groningen, Bologna, Bratislava).
- Local students are matched with incoming exchange students to help them in the first weeks (city tour, introductions, practical help).
- Some add gamification — e.g. ESN Lugano's "Buddy Awards", where groups earn points for meeting up and attending events.
- **Relevance:** Human, high-trust solution; doesn't scale perfectly and depends on volunteers. Our website could complement or connect to such a programme.

## 3. Local "competitors" at unibz / Bolzano

These are what our target users actually use today.

| Solution | What it offers | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| **unibz Exchange Students' Guide (PDF)** | "Your first steps at unibz": arrival, accommodation, health insurance, visa, residence permit, health care, language courses | Static, long document; must be read in full; not interactive or personalised |
| **unibz Incoming Exchange Students web pages** | Step-by-step pages per programme; info on campuses and getting around | Spread across many pages; focused on admin steps |
| **Welcome Session** | Student ID card and unibz network credentials handed out; general introduction | One-off event; information is easy to forget |
| **Study Guide (guide.unibz.it)** | General student information hub, Freshers Days, life on campus | Not tailored to exchange students |
| **Exchange Students unibz (student association)** | Helps exchange students settle in; organises cultural events and excursions; Instagram + email | Information spread across Instagram posts, email and word of mouth |
| **Other associations** (SCUB sports club, k!kero, Talía Unilife in Brixen) | Sports, cultural and social activities (e.g. SCUB organises Snowdays) | Not focused on onboarding |
| **Informal channels** (WhatsApp / Facebook groups, old [accommodationunibz blog](http://accommodationunibz.blogspot.be/)) | Housing offers, quick questions, social life | Unstructured, not searchable, unreliable, scam risk for housing |
| **Erasmusu Bolzano pages** | Forum, experiences, accommodation listings | Outdated content |

**Local context worth noting:**
- Bolzano has a serious housing shortage — a major pain point for incoming students.
- unibz has multiple campuses (Bozen-Bolzano city centre, NOI Techpark, Brixen-Bressanone, Bruneck-Brunico).
- Teaching languages: German, Italian and English.
- Local transport: ABO+ student season ticket for students under 27.

## 4. Competitive gap / positioning

| Competitor type | Local (unibz/Bolzano) specific? | Interactive? | Up to date & maintained? | Covers admin + daily life + social? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Erasmus+ App | No | Yes | Yes | Admin mostly, some social |
| Erasmus Play / Erasmusu | Partly (city pages) | Yes | Erasmusu Bolzano: no | Housing + social |
| Goin' | University-specific, but not used at unibz | Yes | Depends on students | Social mostly |
| Other universities' apps | Their own university only | Yes | Yes | Admin + daily life |
| unibz PDF guide & web pages | Yes | No | Yes | Admin mostly |
| Exchange Students unibz (Instagram) | Yes | Partly | Yes | Events / social |
| WhatsApp / Facebook groups | Yes | Partly | Chaotic | Everything, unstructured |

**Our opportunity:** No existing solution is both **specific to unibz / Bolzano** and **interactive and structured**. Possible differentiators:

- Interactive, Bolzano-specific first-weeks checklist (codice fiscale, residence, ABO+ bus pass, health care, student card, network account…)
- Local knowledge: trilingual context, campuses, transport, where to shop / eat / study
- Housing guidance and scam warnings, given the housing shortage
- One place that gathers information now scattered across a PDF, web pages, Instagram and WhatsApp
- Possible link with Exchange Students unibz events and a buddy-style feature
- Lesson from Eurasmus / Erasmusu: plan for who keeps the content up to date
