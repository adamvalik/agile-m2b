# Desk Research: The First Weeks Abroad

Understand phase, Oct 15, 2026. Adam Valik, Ana Clara Vanzuita

This is secondary research for our context, *the first weeks of exchange students in Bolzano*. It collects numbers from large student surveys, other universities and official sources. Its job is to show that the problem is real, common and worth investigating before we run our own interviews in the observe phase.

**How to read the numbers.** Each figure carries a confidence tag:

- **[Strong]** comes from a large survey (thousands of respondents) or an official source
- **[Indicative]** comes from a smaller, older or secondary source, so use it as support and not as a headline

Surveys differ in year, country and method, so the percentages are not directly comparable with each other.

---

## Pitch in 60 seconds

> Every year about 1.5 million people go abroad with Erasmus+, and Italy is one of the most popular host countries. The first weeks are the hardest part: students have to settle in, sort out paperwork and find their way around a new city all at once. 42% of exchange students say the problems they met abroad caused them anxiety and stress.
>
> Italy makes the start harder. In 2026, expats ranked Italy last for admin topics, and 63% rated Italian bureaucracy negatively, against 39% globally. The steps depend on each other: without a tax code there is no SIM card, no bank account, no rental contract and no transport pass. Non-EU students must also request a residence permit within 8 working days of arrival. Italy is also last worldwide for living without speaking the local language.
>
> Help exists, but students miss it. In Germany only 63% of international students found it easy to find their way on arrival. Over a third of those who skipped orientation didn't know it existed. More than half of exchange students never join a single local activity. Students trust peers who went before them most: 63% want to hear from former exchange students.
>
> Bolzano adds its own layer. unibz has 15.8% international students, three times the Italian average, and a record 186 new incoming students in one semester. Everyday life runs in Italian and German, and the information is spread across PDFs, offices and WhatsApp groups.
>
> So we want to find out where exactly students get stuck in their first weeks in Bolzano, across everything they go through, not only housing.

**Housing in perspective.** Housing is the biggest single pain in the data (35.5% struggle to find affordable accommodation, and over 38% of students going to Italy met a scam). ## 1. Scale: how many people this affects

- **Erasmus+ overall.** In 2024 Erasmus+ gave learning opportunities abroad to almost 1.5 million people, with a budget of €4.7 billion. This counts all sectors, not only universities. [Strong] ([European Commission](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/node/5745))
- **Higher education share.** The consortium behind the Erasmus+ App estimates around 300,000 higher education student mobilities in Europe per year. [Indicative, 2021] ([ELTE](https://elte.hu/en/node/319321))
- **Italy as a destination.** Italy was the 2nd most common host country among the 17,926 exchange students in ESNsurvey 2023 (11.58%), after Spain (12.95%). [Strong] ([ESNsurvey XV](https://erasmusplus.org.ua/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/xv_esnsurvey_-_preliminary_report.pdf))
- **Italy over time.** Between 2014 and 2024, Italy had 1.81 million Erasmus+ participants (inbound and outbound), the second highest after Spain. [Strong] ([data.europa.eu](https://data.europa.eu/en/publications/datastories/crossing-borders-finding-futures-inside-erasmus-mobility))
- **One region as an example.** Lombardy alone hosted almost 9,900 incoming exchange students in 2023/24. [Strong] ([Assolombarda](https://www.assolombarda.it/centro-studi/internazionalizzazione-degli-atenei-di-milano-e-della-lombardia-edizione-2025-rapporto-completo))
- **International students in Italy.** About 96,000 international students were enrolled at Italian universities in 2023/24 (MUR via Statista). Other sources count about 114,000 with a different definition. [Strong] ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1483719/italy-international-university-students))

### unibz specifically

- 4,156 students, **15.8% international** against a national average of 5.3%, from **85 nationalities**. [Strong] ([unibz facts 2024](https://www.unibz.it/en/home/profile/facts-figures/2024))
- **674 exchange spots** and **188 Erasmus+ agreements**, plus 32 bilateral and 6 Swiss (SEMP) agreements. [Strong] (same source)
- **Record intake:** 186 new exchange students in winter semester 2025/26. 84% came through Erasmus+, 9% through bilateral agreements and 7% as free movers, from Europe as well as Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, Ghana, Israel, Malaysia, Taiwan and the US. [Strong] ([unibz magazine](https://www.unibzmagazine.it/en/magazine/article/welcome-event-for-186-new-incoming-students-a-new-record))
- **4 teaching languages** (English, German, Italian, Ladin) on 3 locations (Bolzano, Bressanone, Brunico). Outside Engineering and Agriculture, each programme teaches about one third in English, one third in Italian and one third in German. [Strong] ([unibz exchange fact sheet 2026/27](https://www.unibz.it/assets/Documents/2026-02-17-Fact-Sheet-unibz_25_26.pdf))

**What this means for us:** the group is large and growing, and at unibz it's three times more concentrated than at a typical Italian university. We can find enough people to interview and test with.

## 2. What exchange students struggle with

### ESNsurvey 2023 (XV edition), the largest survey of exchange students in Europe

22,775 responses, of which 17,855 were exchange students. [Strong] ([ESNsurvey XV preliminary report](https://erasmusplus.org.ua/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/xv_esnsurvey_-_preliminary_report.pdf), [EAIE summary](https://www.eaie.org/resource/perceptions-of-international-students-key-findings-esnsurvey.html))

| Problem experienced during mobility | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Grant not enough to cover cost of living | 35.63% |
| Difficulty finding affordable accommodation | 35.50% |
| Problems with academic courses | 33.97% |
| Difficulty integrating with local students | 20.11% |
| Discrimination based on personal background | 4.3% |

- **Impact:** 42.3% said the problems caused them **anxiety and stress**, and 22.9% felt isolated or socially excluded.
- **Satisfaction is falling:** the share "very satisfied" with the host institution dropped from 43.6% (2021) to 34% (2023), and "very dissatisfied" rose from 2.1% to 6%.
- **Paper-heavy admin:** only 22.45% said their mobility procedures were done online.
- **Late money:** 26% received their grant more than 30 days after arrival, so the expensive first weeks are often paid out of pocket.
- **Housing and food** make up about 70% of an exchange student's budget.

Note: the report gives 20.11% both for grant delays and for integration problems, which may be a typo in the report.

### Full-degree students have it harder

The same survey shows that international full-degree students, who also arrive at unibz and face the same steps, struggle more [Strong]:

- **Administrative processes:** 21.71% report difficulties, against 10.98% of exchange students (about twice as many)
- **Welcome and orientation events:** only 32% satisfied, against 46% of exchange students
- **Main problems:** affordable accommodation (43.54%), absence of family and friends (36.94%), connecting with the local community (36.60%)

### ESNsurvey 2021 (XIV edition) on integration

More than 11,100 responses. [Strong] ([EAIE summary](https://www.eaie.org/resource/understanding-international-student-needs.html))

- **54.67%** joined no local activity at all (no sports club, association, volunteering or job)
- **17.2%** felt not integrated or totally not integrated, and 29.24% were neutral
- **17.77%** were dissatisfied with accommodation support from the host institution

## 3. The first weeks are where satisfaction is lowest

The **International Student Barometer (ISB)** is the largest global benchmark of international students' experience.

### ISB global results

- **2024 (106,427 students):** the lowest "living" score is **finding accommodation on arrival (49% satisfied)**. Accommodation cost scores 56%, and 16% of students had not organised accommodation before arriving. The lowest "arrival" score is welcome and pickup at the airport or station (79%). [Strong] ([Etio](https://blog.etioglobal.org/blog/identifying-friction-points-in-the-student-journey-findings-from-the-international-student-barometer))
- **2023 (121,000 students):** 29% of international students worldwide (36% in the UK) did not attend any formal orientation. Among UK non-attendees, **34% did not know it existed** and 41% said it ran before they started. [Strong] ([Etio](https://blog.etioglobal.org/blog/international-student-orientation-and-arrival-findings-from-the-international-student-barometer-isb))

### ISB Germany 2022, a useful comparison with a German-speaking setting

8,100 international students at 17 universities. [Strong] ([GATE-Germany / DAAD](https://www.gate-germany.de/files/GATE-Germany-Schriftenreihe-Hochschulmarketing-Band-22-barrierefrei.pdf))

- Only **63%** said it was easy to navigate their new country on arrival
- **36%** did not attend orientation, mostly because they **didn't know about it (36%)** or arrived after it took place (35%)

| Arrival item | Germany | Global benchmark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accommodation and living orientation | 63% | 77% |
| Introduction to clubs and societies | 66% | 78% |
| First night, getting to where I would stay | 68% | 82% |
| Welcome or pickup at airport, station | 59% | 75% |
| Introduction to the local area | 79% | 81% |

- Satisfaction with **making friends from the host country** was 16 points below the global benchmark, and open comments point to language as one cause

**What this means for us:** universities do well on the academic side, but the practical "how do I live here" part of arrival scores worst. Even existing help is missed because students don't know about it or it happens at the wrong time. Both points are relevant to unibz, which has one Orientation Day per semester (25 Sep 2026 for the winter term).

## 4. Italy makes the start harder

### Bureaucracy

**InterNations Expat Essentials 2026** (via [idealista, Aug 2026](https://www.idealista.it/en/news/node/427255)) [Strong]:

- Italy ranked **29th of 31** destinations overall and **last in Admin Topics**
- Only **19%** found Italian bureaucracy easy, while **63%** rated it negatively (global: 39%)
- **32%** worried about bureaucracy before moving (global: 14%)
- **30th of 31** for ease of getting a visa
- Only **44%** rated online administrative services positively (global: 61%)

Expats are not students, but they go through the same offices (Agenzia delle Entrate, Questura, banks, phone shops).

### Language

From the same InterNations 2026 data [Strong]:

- Italy is **last worldwide for ease of living without speaking the local language**
- **58%** say you need Italian to get by (global: 34%)

An older Italy-specific survey of 1,500 Erasmus students in 27 Italian cities (Students? Magazine and ESN Italy, 2008) found 47% rated their Italian as insufficient. 46.6% said English was useless for daily life and only 1.4% called it essential. [Indicative, 2008] ([Vita.it](https://vita.it/?p=225274))

### Steps that depend on each other

Italian university guides describe a chain of steps, where the codice fiscale (tax code) comes first [Strong, several university guides]:

1. **Codice fiscale.** Needed before you can sign a lease, buy a SIM card, open a bank account or get the südtirolmobil transport pass. Some Agenzia delle Entrate offices give it to non-EU students only once their visa is valid. ([University of Florence guide](https://www.development-lm.unifi.it/upload/sub/The%20guide%20for%20International%20Students%20of%20the%20University%20of%20Florence_-1.pdf), [UNISG](https://www.unisg.it/assets/post-arrival-establishing-legal-status-in-italy-1.pdf), [Eurac Welcome Office](https://eurac.edu/it/careers/welcome-office))
2. **Residence permit (non-EU).** The request must be made **within 8 working days** of entering the Schengen area and costs about **€130**. [Strong] ([unibz fact sheet](https://www.unibz.it/assets/Documents/2026-02-17-Fact-Sheet-unibz_25_26.pdf))
3. **SIM card, bank account, lease registration.** All of them ask for the codice fiscale, and banks also ask non-EU students for the permit receipt.
4. **Transport pass.** The südtirolmobil pass is applied for online and requires a codice fiscale. [Team knowledge, Adam, 2026-10-20; add an official link to evidence.md when found] ([unibz guide](https://guide.unibz.it/en/life-on-campus/public-transportation), [südtirolmobil](https://www.suedtirolmobil.info/en/tickets/ticket-finder/suedtirol-pass-abo))

**Waiting for the permit.** Sapienza tells students to expect about 90 days from the Questura appointment ([Sapienza](https://www.uniroma1.it/it/pagina/permesso-di-soggiorno-motivi-di-studio)). The legal target is 60 days, but advocacy groups report systematic delays, often more than 6 months ([Melting Pot, 2025](https://www.meltingpot.org/2025/02/code-ritardi-e-prassi-illegittime-i-quotidiani-abusi-in-questura/)). For a one-semester exchange, the permit may arrive close to the end of the stay. [Indicative]

**What this means for us:** the order of steps matters, and a mistake early on (for example arriving without a tax code) blocks several later steps. This is direct evidence for a **checklist or timeline** module, and for separating **EU and non-EU** paths.

## 5. Housing

Housing is usually arranged months before arrival: only 16% of students find it after arriving, 28% in Italy. It is part of the picture, not our main focus.

- **ESU/ESN International Student Housing report (2023)**, a snap survey with about 8,000 to 9,000 answers from November 2022 [Strong, self-selected sample] ([ESU](https://esu-online.org/publications/international-student-housing-report-how-are-exchange-students-navigating-the-housing-crisis/), [EAIE](https://www.eaie.org/resource/research-snapshot-european-student-housing.html)):
  - **25%** of exchange students experienced a housing scam, and for students going to **Italy it was more than 38%** (Finland: 10%)
  - **49%** paid a deposit of more than one month and **41%** lacked information about housing conditions
  - Only 16% found housing after arriving, but **in Italy it was 28%**, so more students in Italy are still searching during their first weeks
  - Almost half can cover less than 50% of their housing costs with their grant
- **HousErasmus+ (2017):** almost half of Erasmus+ students found local housing difficult and 12% reported fraud attempts. [Indicative, older] ([EAIE](https://www.eaie.org/resource/research-snapshot-european-student-housing.html))
- **Italy, 2008:** 66% of Erasmus students had trouble finding accommodation, and 20.8% of them met owners who did not want to rent to foreigners. [Indicative, 2008] ([Vita.it](https://vita.it/?p=225274))

### Bolzano

| Item | Cost | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single room in a student hall | €360 per month (+ €720 deposit) | unibz fact sheet [Strong] |
| Double room in a student hall | €270 per month (+ €540 deposit) | unibz fact sheet [Strong] |
| Private single room in a shared flat | €500 to €600 per month (+ 2 to 3 months deposit) | unibz fact sheet [Strong] |
| Private room, upper end | up to about €800 | Il Dolomiti, 2026 [Indicative, sponsored content] |
| Food and personal items | about €300 per month | unibz fact sheet [Strong] |
| Student transport pass (U26) | €153 per year | unibz guide [Strong] |

- Hall places are limited and EU students get them **first come, first served**, so unibz recommends looking for private accommodation. Non-EU bilateral students are guaranteed a place because they need housing for their visa. ([unibz fact sheet](https://www.unibz.it/assets/Documents/2026-02-17-Fact-Sheet-unibz_25_26.pdf))
- For comparison, the average student room costs €714 in Milan, €651 in Bologna and €549 in Trento (Immobiliare.it, April 2025). [Strong] ([Il Post](https://www.ilpost.it/2025/04/23/aumento-affitti-stanze-studenti-citta-universitarie/))

## 6. Loneliness and wellbeing

- **ESNsurvey 2023:** 42.3% felt anxiety and stress because of problems abroad, and 22.9% felt isolated. [Strong]
- **Australia (Sawir et al., 2008):** in 200 interviews, **65%** of international students reported loneliness or isolation, especially in the **early months**. [Indicative, older] ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249632072_Loneliness_and_International_Students_An_Australian_Study))
- **ISB Germany 2022:** making friends with locals is the weakest social point, 16 points below the global benchmark. [Strong]
- **ESNsurvey 2021:** 54.67% joined no local activity. [Strong]
- **Evidence on solutions:** buddy schemes and early peer support show positive effects in small qualitative studies, and peer programmes seem to work best right after arrival. There is no strong controlled evidence yet. [Indicative] ([Brooks 2024, JLDHE](https://journal.aldinhe.ac.uk/index.php/jldhe/article/view/1456))

## 7. Information is spread out, and peers are trusted most

- **Peers:** before departure, **63%** of students want to hear from former exchange students, and 66% want programme information and help with the application. [Strong] (ESNsurvey 2023)
- **Social media:** 82% of international students use social media before they even contact a university. [Indicative, about 2017] ([Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/student/blogs/how-international-students-use-social-media-choose-uk-university)) In a study of 180 international students, 97% used social networking sites to look for everyday information. [Indicative] ([Sin & Kim, 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S074081881300008X))
- **Official tools are underused:** ESN calls the Erasmus+ App the digital tool most in need of strengthening. It had about 120,000 downloads by 2021, against roughly 300,000 mobile students per year. [Indicative] ([ESNsurvey XV](https://erasmusplus.org.ua/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/xv_esnsurvey_-_preliminary_report.pdf), [ELTE](https://elte.hu/en/node/319321))
- **Help is missed:** in the UK, 34% of students who skipped orientation didn't know about it, and in Germany it was 36%. [Strong] (ISB)
- **Housing information:** 41% lacked information about housing conditions. [Strong] (ESU/ESN 2023)

## 8. EU and non-EU students face different first weeks

| Step | EU student | Non-EU student |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry | ID card is enough | Type D study visa from a consulate, with long processing times |
| Residence permit | Not needed | Request within 8 working days, about €130, waiting time of months |
| Health | European Health Insurance Card | Private insurance for the whole stay |
| Student hall | First come, first served | Guaranteed for bilateral students (needed for the visa) |
| Codice fiscale | Needed | Needed, and may only be issued once the visa is valid |

Sources: [unibz fact sheet 2026/27](https://www.unibz.it/assets/Documents/2026-02-17-Fact-Sheet-unibz_25_26.pdf), [University of Lethbridge partner page](https://ulethbridge.ca/node/11894), [Eurac Welcome Office](https://eurac.edu/it/careers/welcome-office)

**What this means for us:** an EU and a non-EU student need different checklists. This supports the EU/non-EU switch from our mockup.

## 10. Gaps and hypotheses for the observe phase

We found **no published data** on:

- Bolzano or unibz exchange students specifically (satisfaction, problems, time spent on procedures)
- How long the codice fiscale, permit and transport pass actually take in Bolzano
- How the bilingual setting affects everyday tasks for newcomers
- Which sources unibz students actually use, and in what order

These gaps are our opportunity. Our interviews will produce the first local data.

**Hypotheses to test** (taken from the evidence above, not yet confirmed for Bolzano):

1. Students lose the most time on steps that depend on each other (codice fiscale before SIM, bank, lease, transport pass)
2. Non-EU students experience the first weeks as much more stressful than EU students
3. Students rely on WhatsApp groups and peers more than on official guides
4. Practical daily-life information (shops, gyms, pharmacies near housing) is missing more than bureaucratic information
5. Students who arrive after Orientation Day miss key information
6. Language (Italian and German) is a barrier for everyday tasks, not for studying

## Caveats

- ESN and ESU surveys are run by student organisations and use self-selected samples. Countries with stronger promotion, such as Italy and Spain, have more respondents.
- The ISB covers international students in general (mostly full-degree), not only exchange students.
- InterNations surveys expats, not students.
- Several Italy-specific sources are old (2008) or from secondary websites, and are marked [Indicative].
- The census figures on language groups in Bolzano were only found in secondary sources, so they are left out. To use them, check ASTAT's 2024 language group census directly.
