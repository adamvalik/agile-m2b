## Summary for the guide

- **The unibz Study Guide pages on banking do not apply to exchange students.** Both say: "If you are an exchange student, this information does not apply to you." [1][2] The unibz source written for exchange students is the **Exchange Students' Guide**. [14]
- An Italian bank account is **optional**: the Exchange Students' Guide lists what is needed to open one, but does not say students must. [14]
- Students with a **euro account in another SEPA country** can use it in Italy: companies and employers must accept euro IBANs from other SEPA countries. [3]
- The **transport pass** does not need an Italian account: it can be paid by card or pagoPA. [4]
- For exchange students, unibz lists a **copy of the passport**, a **certificate of enrolment or self-declaration** and an **Italian tax code**. [14] The banks checked ask for an identity document and a tax code. [7][8][10][11]
- **Non-EU students** also need their **residence permit or the post office receipt** for the permit application. [14]
- The guide must not recommend a specific bank (#18).

## When an account is actually needed

- **For study grants:** the unibz Study Guide says an Italian account is useful to obtain study grants [1], and its non-EU page says students will have to open one for this [2]. Both pages say they do not apply to exchange students, and the Exchange Students' Guide does not link grants to an Italian account. [14] Whether exchange students ever need one for grants is **unknown**.
- **For rent, bills and direct debits:** a company or employer cannot insist on a local account for euro transfers. Under the SEPA Regulation (art. 9), they must accept a euro IBAN from another SEPA country. [3]
- **For the transport pass:** payment by SEPA direct debit, Visa or Mastercard, or pagoPA. [4]

- Banks must identify every customer and must refuse to open an account if they cannot (anti-money-laundering law, D.lgs. 231/2007, art. 42). [5]
- The Italian Banking Association guide says that people who are not tax residents in Italy only need to give a tax code if they already have one. [12] unibz [14] and the banks checked below still list one, so the guide should treat it as required.

- Intesa Sanpaolo accepts a passport explicitly, and the Italian Banking Association guide says foreign citizens can use a passport or ID card. [7][12] isybank is the exception: it accepts only Italian documents. [9]

## Rights under EU law

- People **legally resident in an EU country** have the right to a basic payment account. A bank cannot refuse it just because the person does not live in that country. [6]
- A bank may still refuse if anti-money-laundering rules are not met, if the person already has a similar account in the same country, or, in some countries, if there is no genuine interest in an account there. [6]
- The page does not say whether non-EU nationals living legally in the EU are covered. [6]

## Usual routes for students

- The unibz Study Guide suggests comparing banks' offers and opening an account at the **start of the academic year**, and says most banks offer **student or youth accounts**, usually for people under 26. [1] This page is for degree-seeking students; the Exchange Students' Guide does not cover it. [14]
- Online-only accounts may require Italian residence, so a branch is the more likely route for exchange students. [9]

## Source quality

- **Official:** sources [1], [2], [4], [6] and [14]. Source [3] is a national central bank.
- **The banks' own pages:** sources [7] to [11] and [13].
- **Italian Banking Association guide [12]:** from 2018; it mentions only the residence permit, not the post office receipt.
- **Legal website [5]:** used only for the anti-money-laundering rule.

The unibz Study Guide pages [1] and [2] are for degree-seeking students and say that they do not apply to exchange students. They are used here only where the Exchange Students' Guide [14] says nothing, and are marked as such. The Exchange Students' Guide is the unibz source for exchange students, but other chapters of it are out of date (it still describes the abo+ transport pass at €150).
