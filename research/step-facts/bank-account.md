## Summary for the guide

- unibz describes an Italian bank account as **optional**: useful for managing money locally and for receiving study grants. [1]
- Students with a **euro account in another SEPA country** can use it in Italy: companies and employers must accept euro IBANs from other SEPA countries. [3]
- The **transport pass** does not need an Italian account: it can be paid by card or pagoPA. [4]
- To open an account, banks ask for an **ID card or passport** and an **Italian tax code**. [7][8][10][12]
- **Non-EU students** may also need a **valid residence permit**. [12]
- For a student account, unibz also lists the **unibz Student Card**. [1][2]
- The guide must not recommend a specific bank (#18).

## When an account is actually needed

- **For study grants:** unibz says an Italian account is useful to obtain study grants. [1] Its page for non-EU degree-seeking students says they will have to open one for this. [2] Whether this applies to exchange students is **unknown**.
- **For rent, bills and direct debits:** a company or employer cannot insist on a local account for euro transfers. Under the SEPA Regulation (art. 9), they must accept a euro IBAN from another SEPA country. [3]
- **For the transport pass:** payment by SEPA direct debit, Visa or Mastercard, or pagoPA. [4]

- Banks must identify every customer and must refuse to open an account if they cannot (anti-money-laundering law, D.lgs. 231/2007, art. 42). [5]
- The Italian Banking Association guide says that people who are not tax residents in Italy only need to give a tax code if they already have one. [12] The banks checked below still ask for one, so the guide should treat it as required.

## Rights under EU law

- People **legally resident in an EU country** have the right to a basic payment account. A bank cannot refuse it just because the person does not live in that country. [6]
- A bank may still refuse if anti-money-laundering rules are not met, if the person already has a similar account in the same country, or, in some countries, if there is no genuine interest in an account there. [6]
- The page does not say whether non-EU nationals living legally in the EU are covered. [6]

## Usual routes for students

- unibz suggests comparing banks' offers and opening an account at the **start of the academic year**. [1]
- Most banks offer **student or youth accounts**, usually for people under 26. [1]
- Online-only accounts may require Italian residence, so a branch is the more likely route for exchange students. [9]
