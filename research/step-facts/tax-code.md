## Summary for the guide

- The Italian tax code (**codice fiscale**) is a 16-character code that identifies a person in all dealings with Italian public offices. The only valid code is the one issued by the Agenzia delle Entrate (Italian Revenue Agency). [2]
- **It comes first.** The transport pass and (at the banks checked) a bank account need it. [14][15]
- **EU students can get it from home**, through the Italian consulate or embassy in their country of residence. At the embassies checked, the request is a virtual appointment on prenot@mi and the certificate arrives by email in about 15 days. [10][11][12]
- **In Bolzano, it is by appointment only.** Walk-ins are not served. The appointment is booked online, and the certificate is handed over at the counter. [1][7][9]
- **Foreigners must apply in person.** The Agenzia's page for foreign citizens says a first code needs an in-person appointment so that the office can check the applicant's identity. [1] Requests by email or through a delegate are therefore **disputed** (see routes 2 and 4).
- The wait for an appointment can be **2 to 4 weeks**, according to unibz, so booking from home before arrival helps. [14]
- **What to bring:** form AA4/8 filled in, and a valid passport or ID card. Non-EU citizens bring a passport with visa (or another accepted document) and proof that they may stay in Italy. [1][5][6]
- **Non-EU students** staying more than 90 days normally get the code from the **Questura** when they apply for the residence permit. [1][10][12] Whether they can, or should, get it earlier from the Agenzia office in Bolzano is **unknown**.
- No official page lists a fee for the code or the certificate. [1][2][11][12]

## Name

- Italian: **codice fiscale**. German: **Steuernummer**. English pages of the Agenzia delle Entrate: **tax code** or **tax identification number**. [1][14]
- The plastic card is the **tesserino del codice fiscale**. People registered with the Italian national health service (SSN) get a **tessera sanitaria** (health card), which also shows the code. [2]
- The document students actually use is the **certificato di attribuzione del codice fiscale** (certificate of attribution). It is issued once the code is assigned and can be used while waiting for a card. [2][3]

## Who issues it

| Situation | Who assigns the code | Source |
|---|---|---|
| EU citizen in Italy | any Agenzia delle Entrate office, by appointment | [1][2] |
| Non-EU citizen applying for or renewing a residence permit | the Questura (police headquarters), through a link to the tax register | [1][2][4][5] |
| Non-EU citizen in other situations | an Agenzia delle Entrate office, with a request form | [1] |
| Person living abroad | the Italian consulate or embassy in their country of residence | [2][4][10] |

- For study stays, the Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs says: a stay **under 90 days**, apply at any Agenzia office; a stay **over 90 days**, the code comes with the residence permit application at the Questura. [10] The Embassy in Berlin says the same for non-EU students. [12]
- The Agenzia FAQ says foreign citizens with a residence permit do not need to go to an Agenzia office, because the Ministry of the Interior assigns the code through the Questura. [4]

## Routes from home (before arrival)

### 1. Italian consulate or embassy in the country of residence

- Residents abroad can request the code from the Italian consular office responsible for their place of residence, with the form, a copy of an identity document and the reason for the request. [10]
- Each consulate has its own procedure. The Ministry says to check the website of the competent consular office. [10]
- **Two examples checked:**

| Embassy | Channel | Documents | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vienna (Austria) | virtual appointment on prenot@mi; no visit on the day | signed bilingual form, scan of passport or ID card | digital certificate by email, roughly within 15 days of the chosen date | [11] |
| Berlin | virtual appointment on prenot@mi; no visit on the day | signed form (uploaded under "altro"), scan of passport or ID card | digital certificate by email, roughly within 15 days | [12] |

- Vienna says this route is for EU and non-EU citizens. [11] Berlin describes it for EU citizens; for non-EU students it points to the Agenzia (under 90 days) or the Questura (over 90 days). [12]
- Neither page lists a fee for the certificate. Vienna charges €6.90 only if the plastic card is posted to Austria. [11]
- **The Fast It portal is not for foreigners.** Its tax code service is for Italian citizens registered abroad (AIRE). [10]
- Student reports: through a consulate the code came within a week (W44 in the coding sheet); one interviewee did it fully by email with the consulate in Germany (P5).

### 2. Form AA4/8 sent to an Agenzia office by PEC (disputed for foreigners)

- **The two Agenzia pages disagree.** The page for foreign citizens has a notice: EU and non-EU citizens applying for their first code "must book an in-person appointment at the office", and must go to the counter in person so that their identity can be verified. [1] The general Italian page (updated 20 October 2026) still allows a request by PEC, with an ID copy for EU citizens; for non-EU citizens it mentions only an office appointment. [2]
- So this route may not be open to exchange students. Until the Bolzano office confirms, the guide should not offer it as a main route (open question 1).
- Where it applies, the Agenzia accepts form AA4/8, filled in and signed, sent by **PEC** (certified email), with a copy of an identity document. With a digital signature no ID copy is needed. [2][3]
- The certificate comes back through the same channel: by PEC, or collected at the office. [3]
- The PEC address for all offices in the province is `dp.bolzano.gtpec@pce.agenziaentrate.it`. [9]
- Most exchange students have no PEC address. Several exchange reports say they sent the form and an ID scan by **ordinary email** to the Bolzano office, and that it took 2 to 4 weeks (W1, W3, W9 in the coding sheet). The official pages name only PEC, so whether ordinary email is still accepted is **unknown**. The Bolzano office email is `dp.bolzano.utbolzano@agenziaentrate.it`. [9]

### 3. Book the Bolzano appointment from home

- The appointment is booked online. In the section "Assistenza fiscale", choose the card **"Identificativi fiscali, abilitazioni, consegna documenti e istanze"**. [7]
- The booking form has a box **"Prima richiesta di codice fiscale"** (first request for a tax code), meant for people who have no code yet. The "Codice fiscale utente" field is not marked as required, so the booking should work without a code. [8]
- The booking site is in Italian. One interviewee booked two weeks before arrival with the help of an AI chatbot, went on day 1 and got the code in 10 minutes (P3). Two others did not know booking from home was possible and waited about two weeks after arrival (P1, P2).

### 4. A delegate in Italy (disputed)

- The Ministry of Foreign Affairs says a person living abroad can give someone a special power of attorney to submit the form at an Agenzia office in Italy (Ministerial Decree No. 281 of 17 May 2001). [10] The 2013 form instructions also allow a delegate, who brings their own identity document and a copy of the applicant's. [5]
- The Agenzia's current notice for foreign citizens says the applicant must go to the counter in person for a first code. [1] That appears to rule out a delegate. Whether the Bolzano office accepts one is **unknown** (open question 1).

## Route after arrival: Agenzia delle Entrate office

1. **Fill in form AA4/8.** An English version and an editable version exist. [6] In Part A, tick "allocation of tax code" and, as applicant type, **01: non-resident student enrolling at school or university**. [5] Names are written in block letters without special characters (for example Müller becomes MUELLER). [5]
2. **Book an appointment** online: "Assistenza fiscale", card "Identificativi fiscali, abilitazioni, consegna documenti e istanze", then tick "Prima richiesta di codice fiscale". [7][8] First requests need an in-person appointment so that the office can check the applicant's identity. [1]
3. **Go to the office** with the form and the identity document. [1][5]
4. **Get the certificate** at the counter. [3] Students who are not in the SSN can also ask for the plastic card on the same form; it is sent by post. [2][5]

## Waiting times

| Route | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Consulate, student report | within a week | W44 |

| Appointment in Bolzano: student reports | next day (W32); 2 to 3 weeks (W1); about 2 weeks (P1); about 10 days, after being sent away without an appointment (P2) | W1, W32, P1, P2 |
| At the counter, once there | 10 minutes (P3) | P3 |
| Plastic card by post | **unknown**: no official time found for a first card (the 3 to 4 weeks in the Agenzia FAQ is for a replacement health card) | [4] |

Wait times vary a lot. The guide should say "book as early as possible" rather than promise a number.

## Order of steps

- The tax code is the first official step. It is needed for:
  - the **transport pass** (U26, Fix365, Flex): "a valid Italian tax code" [15]
  - a **bank account**, at the banks checked [14]; see `bank-account.md` in PR #58
- For **non-EU students** staying more than 90 days, the residence permit application is where the code is normally assigned. [1][10][12] The order between the two steps is covered in #38 and #41.
- The unibz Study Guide also says the code is needed for enrolment and study grants and should be sent to the Student Office. [13] That page says "If you are an exchange student, this information does not apply to you", so these points apply to **degree-seeking students only** and are not used for the guide.

## Open questions

1. Does the Bolzano office accept a first request from a foreigner **without an in-person visit**: by PEC, by **ordinary email** (form plus ID scan, as several exchange reports describe), or through a **delegate**? The Agenzia's notice for foreign citizens says no [1]; other official pages say PEC or a delegate is possible [2][5][10]. (Ask the office at `dp.bolzano.utbolzano@agenziaentrate.it`.)
2. Do **non-EU exchange students** in Bolzano get the code at the Questura with their permit application, and how long after? Can they get it from the Agenzia earlier, for example to order the transport pass? (Ask the unibz International Relations Office; check with #38.)
3. For non-EU citizens, which document counts as **proof of the right to stay** (visa, unibz letter, post office receipt)? [1]
4. The online booking should work without a tax code, since the "Codice fiscale utente" field is not marked as required and the first-request box exists. [8] Confirm with a test booking up to "Scelta ufficio", without submitting it.
5. Do all consulates in the main home countries of unibz exchange students (Germany, Austria, Spain, France) use prenot@mi for foreigners? Only Vienna and Berlin were checked. [11][12]

## Source quality

- **Official, Agenzia delle Entrate:** [1] to [9] and [16]. Page [2] was updated on 20 October 2026. The AA4/8 instructions [5] date from 2013 but are still the version linked from the forms page [6]. Pages [1] and [2] disagree on whether foreigners can apply without an in-person visit; [1] is the page written for foreign citizens, so the guide follows it until the Bolzano office says otherwise.
- **Official, Ministry of Foreign Affairs and embassies:** [10] to [12].
- **unibz:** [14] (Exchange Students' Guide, file dated 22 January 2026) is written for exchange students. It is partly out of date: it still describes the abo+ pass at €150 and lists older office hours. [13] (Study Guide, EU citizens) is kept for reference only: it says it does not apply to exchange students.
- **Team research** (coding sheet rows W1, W3, W9, W32, W44; interviews P1 to P5): first-hand reports used only for waiting times and practical tips, never as the rule.
