## Summary for the guide

- **EU students** can keep using their home SIM in Italy at home prices ("roam like at home"), but on a long stay their operator may start charging extra. For an extended stay, the EU itself suggests a local SIM. [1]
- **Non-EU students** are not covered by EU roaming rules, so the cost of their home SIM depends on their operator. [1]
- By law, buying an Italian SIM needs an **identity document**. For non-EU citizens, a passport or residence permit is accepted. The law does not mention the tax code. [2]
- In practice, operators often ask for a **tax code**, and the rules differ between operators and between shops and online. [3][4]
- **Watch out: an operator may ask for a passport**, even from EU citizens. This might not happen everywhere, but one online-only operator refused a Slovak national ID card (občiansky preukaz) and asked for a passport instead (team experience). EU students who have a passport may want to bring it, just in case.
- The guide must not recommend a specific operator (#17).

## When an Italian number is useful

- **EU SIM on a long stay:** the operator may check roaming over the previous 4 months. If the student spent more time abroad and used the phone more abroad than at home, the operator can warn them. If usage doesn't change within 2 weeks of the warning, surcharges can apply, for example €1.30 per GB of data plus VAT. [1]
- For an extended stay abroad, the EU suggests considering a local SIM. [1]
- **Non-EU SIM:** the EU roaming rules are for SIM cards from EU operators, so the cost of a non-EU SIM depends on its own operator's roaming offer. [1]

## What you need

| Item | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Identity document; the operator keeps a copy and records its type and number | required by law | [2] |
| Non-EU citizens: a valid residence permit, passport, equivalent travel document or other recognition document | required by law (any one of these) | [2] |
| EU citizens: possibly a passport | not required everywhere; one operator refused a Slovak national ID card and asked for a passport | team experience |
| Italian tax code | **unknown**: not in the law, but often asked for by operators | [2][3][4] |
| Italian address | **unknown**: one secondary source says operators ask for one, but that most register a temporary address without proof | [4] |

- If the identity document was lost or stolen, a copy of the police report is accepted instead. [2]
- The identification rule is art. 98-undetricies of the Italian Electronic Communications Code, in force in this form since 12 April 2025. [2]

## Usual routes for students

- **In a shop** of a mobile operator. One secondary source says shops are more flexible than online activation when the customer has no tax code. [4]
- **Online:** activation needs remote identification, which the law allows through a digital identity system. [2] At least one operator identifies new online customers by video call. [7] Whether new students without Italian digital ID can use the other methods is **unknown**.
- Whichever route they choose, students should be prepared for an operator not to accept a national ID card from another EU country. It may not happen with every operator, but it has happened (team experience).
- The unibz Study Guide pages for EU and non-EU students do not cover SIM cards. [5][6]

## Source quality

Sources [1] and [2] are official or quote the law, and [7] is an operator's own contract. Sources [3] and [4] are consumer websites and partly contradict each other, so the tax code and address answers stay **unknown** until checked with operators. The passport requirement comes from team experience with one operator; no operator page checked lists which foreign ID cards it accepts. [7]
