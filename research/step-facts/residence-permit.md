## Summary for the guide

- **Non-EU students** staying more than three months must request a residence permit (**permesso di soggiorno**) for study. EU students do not need one. [1][3]
- **Deadline: 8 working days** from entry into Italy, by law. [1][9] unibz counts from arrival in the Schengen area [3]; applying as early as possible covers both.
- **How:** pick up the yellow-band **kit** at a post office, fill it in (unibz International Relations Office helps), and hand it in **open** at a post office with a Sportello Amico counter, together with copies of the documents. [2][3]
- The post office gives a **receipt** (unibz calls it a temporary permit) and a **letter with the date of the Questura appointment**, where fingerprints are taken. [2][3]
- **Cost: about €116.46** for a permit up to one year: €16 revenue stamp, €30 post office fee, €30.46 for the card and a €40 contribution. [1][2][7] The higher unibz figures look out of date (see Cost). [3]
- **Bring:** 1 revenue stamp (€16, only sold in Italy), copies of all passport pages with personal details, visas and stamps, the unibz enrolment certificate, health insurance (stamped by the embassy), proof of accommodation with the host's ID, and proof of means of support. [3][7]
- **Waiting:** by law the permit must be issued within **90 days** of the application. [9] Elsewhere in Italy students wait much longer, and one exchange student never received the permit during a semester (W10).
- **Travel:** the post office receipt alone is not a travel document. While the **type D study visa** is valid, it allows travel in other Schengen countries (90 days in any 180), like a permit. [3][10]

## Who needs it

- Non-EU citizens arriving for the first time. [1]
- Stays of **up to three months** for study do not need a permit; a declaration of presence (dichiarazione di presenza) is made instead. [1] Most exchange semesters are longer than three months, so most non-EU exchange students need the permit.
- A study permit lasts **up to one year** and matches the validity of the entry visa. [1]
- EU citizens do not need a permit. [3]

- **The rule counts from entry into Italy.** Only unibz says Schengen. A student who lands in another Schengen country first is still within the rule if they count from entering Italy, but the guide should advise applying in the first days anyway.

## Steps through to the receipt

1. **Pick up the kit** at any post office. Each person can take one kit and must show an identity document. [2] unibz says to get the form at a post office and then book an appointment with the unibz International Relations Office to fill it in together. [3]
2. **Buy a €16 revenue stamp** (marca da bollo) at a tobacconist. It is only sold in Italy. [2][3]
3. **Pay the postal slip** for the card (€30.46) and the contribution (€40), on one slip. [1][2][7]
4. **Put the documents in the envelope, without sealing it.** unibz says "Do not seal the envelope!" [3] Poste also asks for the envelope open. [2]
5. **Hand it in in person** at an authorised post office (Sportello Amico), with a valid passport. Only the applicant can hand it in. The €30 post office fee is paid at the counter. Booking a slot on poste.it or the Poste app can save time. [2]
6. **Keep the receipt.** unibz calls it a "temporary residence permit" that must be carried with the passport. [3] It also has a user ID and password for tracking the application. [2][3]
7. **Note the Questura appointment** printed on the letter given with the receipt. If no slot can be set, a registered letter with the date is sent to the Italian address on the form. [2][3]

## After the receipt

1. **Questura appointment:** fingerprints are taken. unibz says to bring: passport and visa (originals), the appointment letter, the postal receipts, **4 passport photos** on a light background, and **1 copy** of everything put in the envelope. [3] The Polizia di Stato also asks for 4 photos. [1]
2. **Track the status** online on questure.poliziadistato.it (after the Questura appointment) and on PortaleImmigrazione.it (while Poste handles it). Poste's free number is 800.200.309. [2][3]
3. **Collect the permit** at the Questura once it is ready. unibz student helpers can go with the student on request. [3]
4. **Renewal:** at least **90 days** before it expires, since 4 June 2026 (D.lgs. 83/2026). [9] The unibz guide still says 30 days, which is out of date. [3]

- **Health insurance:** unibz says the policy must be in the student's name, cover the whole stay (the visa period) and carry the embassy's stamp of approval. Without it, "no Residence Permit will be issued". unibz names an emergency-only policy that can be bought in Italy as enough for the application. [3]
- **Means of support:** the national kit list for study permits asks for "Documentation certifying means of support". [7] unibz does not list it. [3] For the study visa in 2026/27, embassies ask for proof of at least €10,179.85 a year. [8] What the Questura di Bolzano accepts (bank statement, grant letter, the visa documents) is **unknown**.
- The national kit list also asks for a self-certification of enrolment and, on renewal, the transcript of exams taken. [7]
- **Tax code:** the permit card shows the tax code [1], and the code is normally assigned at the Questura with the permit application (see `tax-code.md`, #37).

- unibz gives two higher figures, both out of date: about **€137.50** for the procedure, and **€153.50 to €173.50** in its "Cost of Living" section. [3] The second range matches the old €80 and €100 contributions in a 2012 Questura di Bolzano notice. [6]

- The €50 contribution (slip €80.46) applies to permits over 1 and up to 2 years. [1][7] A study permit lasts up to one year. [1]

## Travel with the receipt

- **The receipt alone is not a travel document.** unibz: "In order to be permitted to travel outside Italy you need a final residence permit; the receipt of the post office is not sufficient." [3]
- **The type D visa is.** Under Regulation (EU) 265/2010, a long-stay national visa has the same effect as a residence permit for free movement: while it is valid, the holder can travel and stay in other Schengen countries for 90 days in any 180. [10] A student whose study visa covers the semester can therefore travel within Schengen on passport and visa while waiting for the card.
- Re-entering Italy after a trip outside Schengen (for example home for Christmas) depends on the visa still being valid and on its number of entries; this is **unknown** for a given visa and should be checked on the visa itself. One interviewee was worried about exactly this for a December trip home (P2).
- The older Polizia di Stato notice on travelling with a first-issue receipt covers only work and family reunification permits, and dates from 2007 to 2010. [4] It is historical and does not apply to study permits.
