## Summary for the guide

- **Non-EU students** staying more than three months must request a residence permit (**permesso di soggiorno**) for study. EU students do not need one. [1][3]
- **Deadline: 8 working days** from entry. The Polizia di Stato counts from entry into Italy; unibz says from arrival in the Schengen area. Counting from the first Schengen entry is the safe reading. [1][3]
- **How:** pick up the yellow-band **kit** at a post office, fill it in (unibz International Relations Office helps), and hand it in **open** at a post office with a Sportello Amico counter, together with copies of the documents. [2][3]
- The post office gives a **receipt** (temporary permit) and a **letter with the date of the Questura appointment**, where fingerprints are taken. [2][3]
- **Cost: about €116.46** for a permit up to one year: €16 revenue stamp, €30 post office fee, €30.46 for the card and a €40 contribution. unibz gives about €137.50. [1][2][3][7]
- **Bring:** 1 revenue stamp (€16, only sold in Italy), copies of all passport pages with personal details, visas and stamps, the unibz enrolment certificate, health insurance (stamped by the embassy) and proof of accommodation. [3]
- **Waiting:** the official average is 60 days from the application. [1] Elsewhere in Italy students wait much longer, and one exchange student never received the permit during a semester (W10).
- The receipt is **not enough to travel outside Italy**, according to unibz. [3]

## Who needs it

- Non-EU citizens arriving for the first time. [1]
- Stays of **up to three months** for study do not need a permit; a declaration of presence (dichiarazione di presenza) is made instead. [1] Most exchange semesters are longer than three months, so most non-EU exchange students need the permit.
- A study permit lasts **up to one year** and matches the validity of the entry visa. [1]
- EU citizens do not need a permit. [3]

## Deadline

| Source | Wording | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Polizia di Stato | 8 working days from entry into Italy ("8 giorni lavorativi dal suo ingresso nel territorio dello Stato") | [1] |
| unibz | within 8 working days from arrival in the Schengen area | [3] |

- The two sources disagree on the starting point. A student who enters the Schengen area elsewhere (for example Frankfurt or Vienna) and then travels to Bolzano should count from the first Schengen entry. This is the stricter reading; which one the Questura di Bolzano applies is **unknown**.

## Steps through to the receipt

1. **Pick up the kit** at any post office. Each person can take one kit and must show an identity document. [2] unibz says to get the form at a post office and then book an appointment with the unibz International Relations Office to fill it in together. [3]
2. **Buy a €16 revenue stamp** (marca da bollo) at a tobacconist. It is only sold in Italy. [2][3]
3. **Pay the postal slip** for the card (€30.46) and the contribution (€40), on one slip. [1][2][7]
4. **Put the documents in the envelope, without sealing it.** unibz says "Do not seal the envelope!" [3] Poste also asks for the envelope open. [2]
5. **Hand it in in person** at an authorised post office (Sportello Amico), with a valid passport. Only the applicant can hand it in. The €30 post office fee is paid at the counter. Booking a slot on poste.it or the Poste app can save time. [2]
6. **Keep the receipt.** It works as a temporary permit and must be carried with the passport. It also has a user ID and password for tracking the application. [2][3]
7. **Note the Questura appointment** printed on the letter given with the receipt. If no slot can be set, a registered letter with the date is sent to the Italian address on the form. [2][3]

## After the receipt

1. **Questura appointment:** fingerprints are taken. unibz says to bring: passport and visa (originals), the appointment letter, the postal receipts, **4 passport photos** on a light background, and **1 copy** of everything put in the envelope. [3] The Polizia di Stato also asks for 4 photos. [1]
2. **Track the status** online on questure.poliziadistato.it (after the Questura appointment) and on PortaleImmigrazione.it (while Poste handles it). Poste's free number is 800.200.309. [2][3]
3. **Collect the permit** at the Questura once it is ready. unibz student helpers can go with the student on request. [3]
4. **Renewal:** at least 30 days before it expires. [3]

- **Health insurance:** unibz says the policy must be in the student's name, cover the whole stay (the visa period) and carry the embassy's stamp of approval. Without it, "no Residence Permit will be issued". unibz names an emergency-only policy that can be bought in Italy as enough for the application. [3]
- **Means of subsistence:** for the study visa in 2026/27, embassies ask for proof of at least €10,179.85 a year. [8] Whether the Questura di Bolzano asks exchange students for proof again with the permit is **unknown**; unibz does not list it. [3]
- **Tax code:** the permit card shows the tax code [1], and the code is normally assigned at the Questura with the permit application (see `tax-code.md`, #37).

## Travel with the receipt

- unibz: "In order to be permitted to travel outside Italy you need a final residence permit; the receipt of the post office is not sufficient." [3]
- The Polizia di Stato allows a trip to the home country on a **first-issue receipt** only for work and family reunification permits, through the same border crossing and without transit through another Schengen country. Study permits are not mentioned. [4] That page dates from 2007 to 2010.
- So the guide should say the receipt is **not enough to travel abroad**, and that a trip home before the permit is ready needs checking with the Questura. One interviewee was worried about exactly this for a December trip home (P2).
